Control H' (another plus network): phi 24.2, tuned turing 1.01.

- **H2a** **does not pass**: median Spearman(lambda, phi) = 0.700 (needs >= 0.9); tasks with a lambda whose phi interval is above 90 degrees in every seed: 5 (needs 3). The angle moved by 111.1 degrees in median from lambda = 0 to the largest.

- **H2b** **does not pass**: tasks with a lambda where M is worse alone and the pair better, both intervals excluding zero in every seed: 0 (needs 3).

- **H2c** **passes**: 39 of 39 cases with the phi interval above 90 degrees have the slope of M on a opposite to H's.

**How to read E2.** With lambda > 0 the machines fall below chance: their median d_M is between -1.79 and -1.36, so k is negative (median -0.86 to -0.70). The penalty was satisfied in the cheapest way: the machine turned its own cue upside down. A pair (k, rho) and the pair (-k, -rho) are the same pair, because one only has to believe the machine in reverse. Turned back, these machines meet the person at 14 to 22 degrees, not at 158 to 166. So the angle above 90 degrees is not a gain there: the tuned turing falls from 1.46 at lambda = 0 to 1.09. H2a fails on the rank correlation (the angle jumps at the first lambda and then stays flat), not on the size of the angle; H2b fails because the pair with these machines is worse, not better.

**Exploratory, not in the protocol: the null at positive weight.** For the machines below chance with their interval (38 pairs), the signed turing of the pair should cross zero at w0 = 1/|k| (§ 8 bis). It does in 38 of 38; median w0 / (1/|k|) = 1.015 (quartiles [1.006, 1.024]); median |Tu| at the predicted null, with k from the other half, 0.036. The apparent turing of (k, rho) and of the reversed pair (-k, -rho), measured again with the cue turned over, are the same: median 1.108 and 1.108, largest difference 2.2e-15.

**How to read E2 bis.** The machine trained on the error of the sum did not flip its cue: it learned the frame against the person (slope -0.66, the person's 0.47) and almost nothing of the image (d_a 0.08, k = 0.035). It behaves like the machine that sees only the frame (d_a -0.001, phi 131 against 132 degrees). Its angle with the person is a real one, 132 degrees, and the model predicts the pair: tuned turing 1.37 against 1.36 from k and phi, and 1.34 against 1/sin(phi) = 1.33 for the frame-only machine. A note on the intervals: in 7 of 20 paired differences of the pair's sensitivity the point estimate falls outside its own interval. The point estimate tunes the weight on 49 weights, the resamples on 13 (§ 3), and here the peak is narrow and falls between two of the 13: the intervals sit low. No outcome depends on it: the pair's differences are negative in the point estimates too, and an interval that sits low only makes B4 harder to pass.

**How to read E3.** The angle between people and machine opens as lambda grows: 68 degrees at lambda = 0, 75 at lambda = 3, and the paired intervals exclude zero. But the machine pays for it in accuracy (0.941 to 0.888), so the apparent turing goes down, not up: 1.19 to 1.17. The angle opens because the machine gets worse where people are right, not because it learns what they miss: on the images where at least 98% of the votes are right its accuracy goes from 0.973 to 0.924, and where fewer than 80% are it goes from 0.736 to 0.696 (a description, not in the protocol). At lambda = 10 the network collapses: accuracy 0.186, not far above the 0.1 of chance in ten classes, k = 0.13. The angle of 94 degrees that makes H3b fail belongs to a machine that has stopped working. The group of people with the machine is worth at most 1.03 turings at any lambda.
